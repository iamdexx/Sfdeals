# Contract templates (drafts for attorney review)

These are starting points, not legal advice. Have a local real estate attorney (Sioux Falls or St. Louis, whichever market you are working) review both before the first signature ($300-500, one time). Ask the attorney specifically about:

- The assignment clause and the disclosure to the seller that the buyer may assign.
- Earnest money amount and who holds it (use a title company, never the seller).
- Inspection contingency wording so you can cancel inside the window with the deposit returned.
- SDCL 36-21A-6(6) "pattern of selling" and whether your planned volume needs a license.
- Do not convert either template into an option agreement (36-21A-6(11)).

Title companies in Sioux Falls that handle assignments: ask DakotAbstract, First American Title Sioux Falls, or Midwest Land Title. Confirm they will pay an assignment fee at closing.

## Missouri (St. Louis) notes
- Files: `purchase-agreement-mo.md`, `assignment-mo.md`. Same structure as the SD drafts, Missouri governing law.
- RSMo 339.010 defines a broker as someone acting "for another". Selling or assigning a contract you hold yourself is not brokerage. Only market your contract, never the house, and put the "may assign" language in the purchase agreement.
- Missouri foreclosures are non-judicial trustee sales (RSMo 443.310 onward): about 20 days of newspaper notice, no court case. The owner can sell up to the sale day.
- Disclosures: no statewide seller disclosure form; methamphetamine disclosure is required (RSMo 442.606). Lead paint for pre-1978 homes.
- City of St. Louis has a vacant building fee and a land tax sale (sheriff) a few times a year; back taxes and vacant building fees are paid from the sale proceeds at closing like any lien.
- Title companies in St. Louis that handle assignments: Investors Title, Continental Title, Title Partners Agency. Confirm they will pay the assignment fee at closing.
